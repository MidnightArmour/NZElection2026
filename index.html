Methodology and assumptions

Polling model

Uses fieldwork midpoints, the latest poll from each pollster, recency and sample-size weighting, strongly shrunk current-cycle pollster offsets and uncertainty informed by disagreement between pollsters. Current support is separated from residual election-day error.

Electorate model

Uses full transposed 2023 distributions. General electorates have no complete verified local-poll series, so the model widens their uncertainty rather than allowing one isolated poll to dominate. Māori electorates use the latest available 2026 electorate polls, blended with historical baselines according to poll strength, undecided share and candidate disruption. Te Pāti Māori receives a one-way candidate-vote premium rather than a reciprocal Labour concession. Each simulation still links electorate movement to its realised national two-bloc vote.

MMP allocation

Applies the 5% or electorate-seat eligibility rule, Sainte-Laguë allocation and overhang seats. Allocation logic is checked against fixed threshold and overhang test cases.

Interpretation

Party votes are simulated compositionally in log-ratio space with correlated, heavy-tailed current-support and election-day errors. Frequencies are simulation results under stated assumptions, not historically calibrated probabilities. Strategic-voting profiles are scenarios rather than confidence intervals.

Model outputs are scenario-dependent estimates, not election results or electorate polls.
